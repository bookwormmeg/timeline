A Timeline of the Development of Nintendo Game Systems

1889

Founded as Nintendo Koppai on 23 September 1889 by Fusajiro Yamauchi in Shimogyō-ku, Kyoto, Japan as an unincorporated establishment, Nintendo was created as mainly a toy producer of Japanese playing cards (specifically hanafuda).

1902

Nintendo began manufacturing the first western playing cards in Japan- these were intended for export, but quickly gained popularity in Japan.

1929

Due to the laws in Japan at the time, Sekiryo Kaneda (Fusajiro’s son in law) took the Yamauchi name to continue the Nintendo brand as a family business. At this time, Nintendo was the largest playing card brand in Japan.

1950

Hiroshi Yamauchi, Sekiryo’s grandson, assumed the presidency over Nintendo.

1953

At this time, Nintendo became the first company to succeed in mass-producing plastic playing cards in Japan. However, with the restructuring of the company that followed, some of the workers went on a hunger strike to combat the expansion- this achieved very little as many of the workers were dismissed.

1959

In a collaboration with Walt Disney, Nintendo began producing cards printed with Disney characters, resulting in a surge of children’s cards.

1970

Nintendo began selling the ‘Beam Gun’ series, introducing electronic technology into the toy industry for the first time in Japan.

1974

After creating an image-projection system, the company began exporting these arcade machines into amusement arcades across America and Europe.

1978

In March, Nintendo released ‘Computer Othello’ which was a simplistic arcade game with no joystick and ten coloured buttons per player.

1980

As well as beginning to sell the ‘Game & Watch’ product line in Japan, Nintendo developed the arcade video game "Donkey Kong"- this quickly became the hottest-selling individual coin-operated machine in the business.

1984

Nintendo released the Famicom system (later renamed the Nintendo Entertainment System or N.E.S) which introduced many popular titles including but not limited to Super Mario Bros.

1989/1990

The first portable, handheld game system with interchangeable game cartridges- the Game Boy- is launched along with Tetris.

1996

On February 26th, the first ever Pokémon game was released and later in the year on June 23rd, Nintendo introduced the Nintendo 64 which sold over 500,000 copies on the first day.

2001

•Nintendo UK established in January.
•Pokémon Gold and Silver for Game Boy Color simultaneously launched in Europe on 6 April, sold 1 million copies over the launch weekend.
•Game Boy Advance launched 21 March in Japan, 11 June in the US and 22 June across Europe- it managed to sell 500,000 units in the first week.
•Nintendo GameCube launched 14 September 2001 in Japan and 18 November 2001 in America- sold 2.65 million units total by December.
•Game Boy Advance e-Reader hardware launched in Japan on the 1st of December, meaning information from different games could be stored on cards and then transferred onto the Game Boy Advance.

2002

•Pokémon mini was released as the world’s smallest console at the time.
•The Nintendo Gamecube was released on the 3rd May.
•After 52 years, Hiroshi Yamauchi announced his retirement as president as well as his successor Satoru Iwata.

2006

Towards the end of 2006, the Nintendo Wii was released, both introducing gaming classics such as Wii Sports (and generally Miis) as well as bringing in a whole new audience of fans.

2011

•In early 2011, the Nintendo 3DS was launched.
•To commemorate the anniversary, Nintendo hosted a special one-off concert: The Legend of Zelda 25th Anniversary Symphony Concert Tour.

2017

In March, the Nintendo Switch was released as a hybrid system (meaning it was able to be both a handheld and a home console)- later on, it became the second best selling console of all time behind the Playstation 2 with over 156 million units sold.

2018

In mid-June, Shuntaro Furukawa overtook Tatsumi Kimishima as company president of Nintendo.

2019- 2021 (Covid)

Although the COVID-19 pandemic caused some of Nintendo’s production lines to come to a halt, they reported their income had increased by around 75%. This was especially supported by Nintendo Switch Online and a rising popularity in video games such as Animal Crossing: New Horizons.

2025

On the 5th of June, as the most recent addition to the Nintendo product line, the Nintendo Switch 2 became the fastest selling console in history with over 3.5 million units sold in just five days.

Future

Based on recent game and console releases, Nintendo may be venturing into even broader varieties of entertainment consoles- we may expect to see higher resolutions, more capable performances and sleeker designs as well as virtual reality options in the future.
